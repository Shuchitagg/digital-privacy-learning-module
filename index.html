1. Why digital privacy matters

Digital privacy means having reasonable control over how personal information is collected, shared, stored, and used. Personal information can include your name, location, passwords, photographs, health details, school or work records, and browsing activity.

Key idea: Before sharing information online, pause and ask: “Who needs this, why do they need it, and what could happen if it is shared further?”

2. Build stronger accounts

  • Use a long, unique passphrase for each important account.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication when available.
  • Use a trusted password manager rather than reusing passwords.
  • Review account-recovery information regularly.
  • Never share verification codes with another person.

Scenario: Which password is stronger?

Select the best option.

A longer passphrase made of unrelated words is generally harder to guess and easier to remember than a short password based on familiar personal information.

3. Spot the risk

Email message

Subject: URGENT: Your account will be closed today

“We noticed suspicious activity. Click the link below immediately and enter your password to keep your account active.”

The sender address contains an unfamiliar spelling and the link does not match the organization’s official website.

Which warning signs are present?

Safer response: Do not click the link. Open the organization’s official website or app yourself, check the account there, and report the message through an approved channel.
